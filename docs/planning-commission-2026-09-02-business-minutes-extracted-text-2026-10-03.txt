ZONING MAP AMENDMENT, Corrin Gulick, Chairwoman, Steve Lilly, Staff 
 
1. Request of Kroger (applicant) for Richwood Pointe LLC (owner)  for: ( 1) a 
Zoning Map Amendment from Employment Planned Development (EPD) to 
Commercial Services (C- 3); (2) a Variance reducing the Buffer Yard A 
requirement from ten (10) feet to zero (0) feet along the new internal property line; 
and (3) a Special Sign District, for an approximate 20-acre area located along the 
northeast side of Paddock Drive, between Richwood Road and Stirrup Lane, 
approximately 1,100 feet northwest of Richwood Road, and along the northwest 
side of Frogtown Connector Road, approximately 1,100 feet north of Richwood 
Road, Boone County, Kentucky, having a Parcel Identification Number (PIDN) of 
075.00-00-102.10. The request is for a zone change to allow the development of 
an approximate 99,000 square foot grocery, an approximate 7,565 square foot 
wine and spirits store, a fuel center, and an approximate 51,840 square foot multi-
tenant retail/service building. (Jurisdiction: Boone County) 
 
Staff Member, Steve Lilly, read the Committee Report, which recommended denial based upon 
the Committee Report and Findings of Fact. 
 
At this time, Chairman Rolfsen asked if there was anyone in the audience who wanted to speak 
in favor or against the request. 
 
Mr. Patrick Hughes, DBL Law, stated that he was present on behalf of the applicant and Kroger. 
Both the applicant and Kroger disagree with the Committee’s recommendation. Traffic seems to 
be the focus of the Committee’s recommendation. Neither Boone County Public Works nor KYTC 
has concluded on the record that the roadway network cannot accommodate the proposed 
development. Committee comments focused on operational considerations, access 
configurations, and requests for additional analysis.  In response, Kroger’s traffic engineer 
insured an addendum to the traffic impact study and provided additional analysis to address 
these comments. At no point has any of the traffic impact study been refuted on the record. The 
Committee’s principal concern was the potential for cut -through traffic from Heritage Trails 
Subdivision associated with the proposed Paddock Drive access.  Kroger’s traffic engineer 
reviewed the roadway network of this subdivision and evaluated the five intersections located 
along Paddock Drive.  Based on this analysis, the engineer concluded that each of these 
intersections is expected to continue operating at acceptable levels of service in both the AM 
and PM peak hours through the 2038 horizon year. There is nothing submitted in the record that 
refutes this. The traffic impact study assumes that only approximately 20% of the site traffic will 
utilize the Paddock Drive access, because that traffic is expected to originate primarily from 
Heritage Trails residents and a portion of Triple Crown residents who already use the local 
roadway network. There is no traffic beyond what is already using that roadway.  That is an 
important distinction between existing traffic and new traffic.  Our study shows that the 
development is not adding to traffic along Paddock Road beyond the existing residences. The 
relevant question is not whether every conceivable traffic concern has been eliminated, but 
instead whether competent engineering and evidence demonstrates that the transportation  
 
 
 
 
BOONE COUNTY PLANNING COMMISSION               September 2, 2026 
Business Meeting Page 4 
 
network can function with the proposed improvements and access configuration. This record 
supports all three statutory findings necessary for approval of the zoning map amendment. It is 
consistent with the Comprehensive Plan. There have been changes. The C -3 district is more 
appropriate than the current EPD district. In recent decades the population has more than tripled 
in the surrounding area, increasing demand for amenities such as this proposal.  This proposal 
provides for needed commercial amenities, significant private investment , and service that 
residences of a growing area want and need. 
 
Deborah Cahill stated that she lives in a beautiful subdivision, but Paddock Drive does not have 
any sidewalks and people are forced to walk in the street, and the additional traffic is a concern. 
With the proposed design, cars will turn right onto Paddock D rive and make U -turns or turn 
around in private driveways. 
 
Todd Cleveland stated that he lives on Paddock Drive and the proposed development will create 
more traffic on the roadway.  Traffic from Frogtown Road will come down Paddock Drive to the 
development. The only reason Kroger wants to build now is to compete with Publix. There is no 
need for another Kroger. There are plenty of Kroger stores in the area.  The intersection with 
Frogtown Road will not be able to handle additional traffic. 
 
Greg Lang questioned the need for another grocery store. 
 
Ms. Gulick moved, by Resolution to the Boone County Fiscal Court, to recommend denial 
of the Zoning Map Amendment request based on the Committee Report and Findings of 
Facts. The motion was seconded by Mr. Lunnemann. 
 
Chairman Rolfson asked if the Commission wanted to discuss the issue. 
 
Ms. Gulick stated that the Committee did not dispute the traffic impact study, because the traffic 
study showed that queue lengths at the proposed signal on Frogtown Connector Road could 
extend south past the northern entrance to the truck stop. That was an immediate concern. The 
Committee also discussed the curb cut onto Paddock Drive and the Committee determined that 
this access point was not appropriate due to potential cut-through traffic, U-turn movements, and 
pedestrian safety. Another concern was the orientation of the Kroger building having the truck 
service area/loading docks  between the Kroger building and residences; the concern being a 
noise issue. 
 
Chairman Rolfson stated that the Planning Commission reviews these requests based on land 
use issues and the relevant laws.  Being a free market society, the Planning Commission does 
not review these requests from a marketability viewpoint. 
 
With no further discussion, Chairman Rolfson called for a vote on the motion which 
passed unanimously. 
 
Mr. Szurlinski asked if the Commission had to take action on the requested Variance and Special 
Sign District. Ms. Gulick stated that her motion for denial included those requests as well.  Mr. 
Lunnemann stated that his second on the motion also included those requests. No Commission 
member wanted to change their vote.

Town Overlay (RS/UTO) to Urban Residential One/Planned Development (UR -
1/PD) for an approximate 32.96 -acre area located at 8928, 8936, and 8946 Old 
Union Road, including the property having a Parcel Identification Number (PIDN) 
of 051.00-00-038.01, Boone County, Kentucky. The request is for a Zoning Map 
Amendment to allow a 340 multi-family dwelling unit development, to be annexed 
into the City of Florence. (Jurisdiction: Boone County/Florence) 
   
Staff Member, Michael Schwartz, read the Committee Report, which recommended approval of

At this time, Chairman Rolfsen asked if there was anyone in the audience who wanted to speak 
in favor or against the request? 
 
Ms. Loren Wolf, DBL Law, stated that she is representing the applicant.  The proposal has 
undergone extensive planning over a couple of years with Boone County, City of Florence, and 
KYTC. The record shows that many of the issues that have been raised have all been addressed. 
The City of Florence supports the proposal.  The Boone County School District expressed 
support. KYTC has reviewed the traffic study, and the recommended traffic improvements have 
been incorporated into the plan to mitigate any impacts.  The Zone Change Committee, after 
hearing the evidence and considering the issue, is recommending approval of the request. Only 
one legal finding under KRS 100 is necessary to grant a Z oning Map Amendment. The record 
clearly establishes that the proposal is in agreement with the Comprehensive Plan and that the 
existing zoning is inappropriate, while the proposed zoning is appropriate. The Comprehensive 
Plan must be read as a whole and the direction for this area is not limited to the Union Town 
Plan, which is more than 25 years old and warrants a major update. The Comprehensive Plan 
expressly recognizes development west and south of this area, including areas along US 42, for 
increased densities to promote a larger variety of housing types and offers opportunities for the 
needs of changing household demographics. This language directly supports the request.  The 
proposed project provides for greater housing variety, open space areas, amenities, pedestrian 
connections, intensive buffering, transportation improvements, and right -of-way for future 
roadway development.  The existing zoning is inappropriate,  and the UR -1/PD zone is 
appropriate. The existing zoning does not reflect the development patterns surrounding the site, 
the available infrastructure, or the growth identified in the Comprehensive Plan.  The proposed 
UR-1 zone, by contrast, is specifically intended to create an urban scale residential environment 
for compact housing types on land with adequate infrastructure and access to urban activit y 
centers. That is where the PD really comes in. The Comprehensive Plan sets the direction, and 
the PD is intended to implement that.  Section 1506 expressly provides that additional intensity 
may be justified by a mixed-use development and/or an amenity -oriented development with 
community or recreational facilities. The operative language is and/or. A project does not have 
to be traditional mixed-use if its intensity is independently supported by an amenity -oriented 
design. This proposal does all of that. It provides buffering, environmental preservation, and

impact study. KYTC has accepted not only its conclusions but also its methodology. All of  its 
recommended changes to mitigate the impact have been incorporated into the plan. The study 
included future conditions using the annual growth factor that was accepted by KYTC. There is 
no evidence in the record contradicting that professional analysis . Generalized concerns and

The pre-K issue was raised and discussed at the Committee level, which was answered by the

Plan and that the proposed UR -1/PD zone is appropriate.  Florence supports the project, the

evidence, not speculation.
